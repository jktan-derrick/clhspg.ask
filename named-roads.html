The People Behind the Signs

Streets Named After People

Many of George Town's roads carry the names of real people — founders, chief ministers, lawyers, entertainers and philanthropists. Here is who they were, and when and why a road was named after them.

George Town grew up under British rule, so for a long time its streets honoured European officials. Today, roads named after Malaysians — and even whole new highways — continue the tradition. Two of Penang's best-known examples were both named very recently, in honour of men who shaped modern Penang.

Two Modern Namesakes

Photo: HundenvonPenang · CC BY-SA 4.0
🛣️

Tun Dr Lim Chong Eu Expressway

Lebuhraya Tun Dr Lim Chong Eu · named after Tun Dr Lim Chong Eu (1919–2010)

Lim Chong Eu was the second Chief Minister of Penang, leading the state from 1969 to 1990. He is remembered as the architect of modern Penang: he turned a declining free port into an electronics-manufacturing powerhouse by opening the Bayan Lepas Free Industrial Zone, and he championed the building of the Penang Bridge that first linked the island to the mainland.

When & why

The 17.8 km coastal expressway along Penang Island’s east coast was originally the Jelutong Expressway. After Lim Chong Eu died on 24 November 2010, the Penang state government renamed it in his honour — announced on 26 November and made official on 7 December 2010 — because the highway runs past the Bayan Lepas industrial zone and Penang Bridge, the two projects most tied to his legacy.

Karpal Singh Drive

Persiaran Karpal Singh · named after Karpal Singh (1940–2014)

Karpal Singh was one of Malaysia’s most famous lawyers and politicians, nicknamed the “Tiger of Jelutong” for holding the Jelutong parliamentary seat in Penang for five straight terms. National chairman of the DAP and a fearless defender of civil liberties, he was born and raised in George Town.

When & why

Karpal Singh was killed in a road accident on the North–South Expressway on 17 April 2014. Just two days later, on 19 April 2014, Chief Minister Lim Guan Eng announced that the seafront promenade at Bandar Sri Pinang in Jelutong — then known as the IJM Promenade — would be renamed Karpal Singh Drive, in the very constituency he served for decades. It was officially launched that June.

More Roads Named After People

Across George Town and Penang Island, dozens of roads carry a person’s name. Here are some of the most notable — and the stories behind them.

Photo: Vnonymous · CC BY-SA 4.0

Light Street 1786

Lebuh Light · Captain Francis Light (1740–1794)

The founder of British Penang, who landed in 1786 and named the new settlement George Town. He named this street — the city’s first, beside the Esplanade — after himself.

Gurney Drive 1952

Persiaran Gurney · Sir Henry Gurney (1898–1951)

The seaside promenade (built 1936 as the New Coast Road) was renamed in 1952 after the British High Commissioner in Malaya, who was assassinated during the Malayan Emergency in 1951.

Jalan P. Ramlee 1982

Formerly Caunter Hall Road · Tan Sri P. Ramlee (1929–1973)

Malaysia’s greatest entertainer — actor, singer, director — was born on this Penang street. It was renamed in his honour; his birthplace here is now a memorial.

Macalister Road early 1800s

Jalan Macalister · Colonel Norman Macalister

Named after Norman Macalister, a Governor of Penang from 1807 to 1810. Today it is one of George Town’s busiest commercial roads.

The “Grand Old Man of Penang” — a penniless immigrant barber who became a banker and one of Penang’s richest philanthropists.

Named after the Lieutenant-Governor of Penang (1800–1803). The famous indigo-blue Cheong Fatt Tze Mansion stands on this street.

Jalan Anson · Sir Archibald Anson (1826–1925)

Named after the Lieutenant-Governor of Penang (1867–1882), remembered for improving the island’s roads and public works.

Formerly Scott Road · D. S. Ramanathan

Once named after James Scott, Francis Light’s business partner, it was later renamed after D. S. Ramanathan — the first elected Mayor of George Town (1957).

Why so many European names?

Because Penang was a British settlement from 1786, its early roads were named by and for colonial administrators and merchants. Since independence, many have been given Malaysian names — honouring leaders like Lim Chong Eu and Karpal Singh, or restoring older local names — while the English names often live on in everyday use. Street signs in the core zone usually show both the modern name and the historic one.
