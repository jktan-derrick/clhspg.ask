Your Questions, Answered

🏛️ Heritage Guide

Quick answers to the questions visitors ask most about George Town. Tap a question to open it.

What is George Town and why is it special?
George Town is the historic capital of Penang, Malaysia. In 2008 UNESCO named it a World Heritage Site for its unique multicultural heritage. Read the full history →
What is UNESCO and why does it protect George Town?
UNESCO is the UN's Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization, founded in 1945 to build peace through culture and education. Learn why we have UNESCO →
What is the oldest temple in George Town?
The Goddess of Mercy Temple, built in 1728, is the oldest Chinese temple. Visit it →
What are the "must-see" heritage sites?
Top picks: the Blue Mansion, Khoo Kongsi, Chew Jetty, Kapitan Keling Mosque and the street art. See all 17 sites →
What are the Clan Jetties?
Floating villages of wooden houses built on stilts over the sea by Chinese immigrant clans. Visit Chew Jetty →
How do I get around and find directions?
The old town is small and best explored on foot or by trishaw. Every site has a directions button. Open the Directions page →
Is George Town good for a school or family trip?
Yes! It is compact, safe and full of history, colourful temples, street art and famous food. See the walking route →
What food is George Town famous for?
Penang is one of Asia's great food cities, try char kway teow, assam laksa, cendol and Nyonya dishes. See Little India →

Popular questions
Which is the oldest?
The oldest Chinese temple is the Goddess of Mercy Temple (1728). The oldest Hindu temple is Sri Mahamariamman (1833), and the oldest mosque is the Kapitan Keling Mosque (1801).

All 17 heritage sites
Cheong Fatt Tze Mansion
Chinese · built 1890s
An indigo-blue courtyard mansion of 38 rooms, a masterpiece of Straits-Chinese architecture and a UNESCO award-winning restoration.
Read the full story →  ·  Directions
Khoo Kongsi
Chinese · built 1906
The grandest Chinese clan house in Malaysia, a richly carved and gilded temple built by the Khoo family from Fujian.
Read the full story →  ·  Directions
Cheah Kongsi
Chinese · built 1873
One of the oldest Straits-Chinese clan associations in Penang, set behind a peaceful garden courtyard off Armenian Street.
Read the full story →  ·  Directions
Goddess of Mercy Temple
Chinese · built 1728
Kuan Yin Teng, the oldest Chinese temple in George Town, always wreathed in incense and dedicated to the Goddess of Mercy.
Read the full story →  ·  Directions
Han Jiang Ancestral Temple
Chinese · built 1870
An award-winning Teochew clan temple whose delicate carvings earned a UNESCO conservation prize.

Chew Jetty
Chinese · built 1890s
A century-old stilted water village where the Chew clan still live in wooden houses built out over the sea.

Pinang Peranakan Mansion
Chinese · built 1890s
A lavishly restored Baba-Nyonya mansion packed with over a thousand antiques of the Straits-born Chinese.

Sun Yat Sen Museum
Chinese · built 1910
The modest blue shophouse that served as Dr. Sun Yat Sen's Southeast Asian base for China's 1911 revolution.

Sri Mahamariamman Temple
Indian · built 1833
The oldest Hindu temple in Penang, ablaze with hundreds of sculpted deities across its towering gopuram.

Little India
Indian · built 1800s
A few vivid blocks of sari shops, garland sellers and banana-leaf restaurants, the beating heart of Indian George Town.

Nagore Dargha Sheriff
Indian · built 1801
A striking green-and-white shrine built by South Indian Muslims to honour a Sufi saint, a rare Indo-Islamic gem.

Kapitan Keling Mosque
Malay-Muslim · built 1801
George Town's oldest and grandest mosque, founded by Indian-Muslim traders, its golden domes crowning the Street of Harmony.
